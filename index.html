Dusting the Phantom: Gaussian Allocation for Fading Dynamic Content

Minhyeok Roh1, Minseo Lee1, Jun Young Jeong2, Byeonghyeon Lee1, Lucas Yunkyu Lee3, Jaesik Park4, Eunbyung Park1
1 Yonsei University
2 Electronics and Telecommunications Research Institute
3 POSTECH
4 Seoul National University
arXiv

Fast motion should not become a phantom. We allocate Gaussian capacity to under-reconstructed dynamic regions—and place it at the time it is needed.

01

Abstract

Dynamic Gaussian representations have made high-quality, real-time volumetric video increasingly practical, yet challenging motion can still break their reconstruction: a small fast-moving object may partially disappear, and fine textures in highly dynamic regions are often lost. Surprisingly, simply training longer or using more Gaussians does not reliably resolve these failures. We argue that the key bottleneck is not representational capacity itself, but where and when that capacity is allocated. We introduce two complementary strategies for improved spatiotemporal Gaussian allocation. First, Seed Injection introduces new Gaussians directly from multi-view observations, using temporal appearance changes and reconstruction errors to identify under-reconstructed dynamic regions and place new support at geometrically plausible 3D locations. This allows the model to recover content that may have little or no existing Gaussian support. Second, Temporal Biased Relocation preserves time-resolved optimization signals and uses them to guide when and where relocated Gaussian capacity should be reassigned. Both strategies can be incorporated into existing dynamic Gaussian frameworks without modifying their underlying scene representation. Experiments across multiple dynamic-scene datasets show consistent improvements in reconstruction quality, with particularly pronounced gains, including better recovery of small, rapidly moving objects and fine-grained appearance details and textures.

Gaussian Allocation, Reconsidered

Our method addresses both missing spatial support and poorly chosen temporal destinations.

01SPACE

Seed Injection

A dynamic error-based seed map combines temporal intensity changes with reconstruction error. Sampled pixels are lifted through synchronized multi-view rays, producing geometrically plausible 3D seeds—even where no suitable Gaussian currently exists.

The image above shows actual Seed Injection regions in our custom dataset, with red markers indicating where new Gaussians were injected. By targeting regions with both dynamic motion and high reconstruction error, Seed Injection allocates more Gaussians to areas that need additional support. These regions can be missed by conventional relocation, which relies on existing Gaussians as relocation targets. Seed Injection introduces new support directly into such regions, even when no suitable Gaussian is available to guide relocation.

02TIME

Temporal Biased Relocation

FreeTimeGS reuses low-opacity Gaussians but pays limited attention to when additional capacity is needed. We preserve the target Gaussian’s timestamp-specific positional gradients and select the time with the largest gradient magnitude as the moment requiring the strongest additional support. The relocated Gaussian is centered at that timestamp and the target’s corresponding 3D position, with its temporal duration adjusted accordingly. By allocating Gaussian support to the moments that need it most, Temporal Biased Relocation improves reconstruction quality in dynamic regions and increases overall PSNR.
