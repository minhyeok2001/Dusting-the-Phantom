Dusting the Phantom: Gaussian Allocation for Fading Dynamic Content

Fast motion should not become a phantom. We place Gaussians where and when dynamic content needs them.

01

Abstract

Dynamic Gaussian representations enable high-quality, real-time volumetric video, yet small, fast-moving objects and fine textures can still disappear. More training or more Gaussians does not reliably solve this problem: the bottleneck is where and when capacity is allocated. Seed Injection uses temporal appearance changes and reconstruction errors to identify under-reconstructed dynamic regions, then introduces new Gaussians at geometrically plausible 3D locations from multi-view observations. Temporal Biased Relocation uses time-resolved optimization signals to guide when and where existing Gaussian capacity is reassigned. Both strategies preserve the underlying scene representation. Experiments across dynamic-scene datasets show improved reconstruction quality, especially for small, rapidly moving objects and fine textures.

Gaussian Allocation, Reconsidered

Allocate Gaussians where and when they are needed.

01SPACE

Seed Injection

Temporal appearance changes and reconstruction errors identify dynamic regions that need more support. Synchronized multi-view rays guide the placement of new Gaussians at geometrically plausible 3D locations.

Red markers show actual Gaussian injection locations in our custom dataset. Seed Injection adds support to dynamic regions with high reconstruction error, including areas that conventional relocation can miss because they lack suitable existing Gaussians.

02TIME

Temporal Biased Relocation

We use timestamp-specific positional gradients to relocate low-opacity Gaussians to the time of greatest need. Each relocated Gaussian adopts the target’s 3D position at that time, with its temporal duration adjusted accordingly. This improves reconstruction in dynamic regions while also enhancing overall reconstruction quality.
